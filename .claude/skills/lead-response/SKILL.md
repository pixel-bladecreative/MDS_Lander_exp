# Lead Response

Leads contacted within 5 minutes close at 25–32%. After an hour, 3–5%. The
median dealer takes 47 minutes. **This skill exists to make you the person who
answers in four.**

Getting a good message out in 90 seconds beats a perfect one in twenty minutes.
Do not let the user polish. Give them something sendable immediately.

---

## Step 1 — Consent gate. Never skip this.

Before drafting anything, establish which bucket this is:

| Situation | Text OK? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| They just submitted a form / messaged first | **Yes** | They initiated. Reply freely. |
| Existing customer, one-to-one, conversational | **Yes** | Keep it personal, honor opt-out instantly. |
| Aged lead, no opt-out on file, personal note | **Ask the user to confirm CRM consent field first** | |
| Any list, any blast, any promotion without written consent | **No — refuse and say why** | $500–$1,500 per message. |
| Marked opted out | **No. Ever.** | |

If the user has not said which bucket it is, ask in one line before drafting.
If it is a prohibited bucket, say so plainly, explain in one sentence, and
offer the compliant alternative — usually a phone call.

## Step 2 — Ask only what you actually need

If the user pasted a lead, work with what is there. Do not interrogate. The
only things that change the message:

- Name, and the specific vehicle (year/trim/stock# if available)
- Source (website form, Marketplace, phone, walk-in, referral)
- Anything they actually said in their own words
- Whether they are an existing customer

Missing pieces are fine. Write the message and mark the gap as `[confirm]`.

## Step 3 — Write it like one person talking to one person

This is the whole thing. Generic dealership copy is why nobody replies.

**Rules:**
- **Under 40 words for a first text.** Under 90 for an email.
- **Use their words back to them.** If they said "something safe for my
  daughter," the reply is about their daughter, not about "our inventory."
- **One question, not three.** Multiple questions get zero answers.
- **Offer two specific times,** never "when works for you?" — that is work you
  are handing the customer.
- **Sign with a real name.** Never "Sales Team."
- **No exclamation points stacked, no "Great news!", no "I hope this email
  finds you well."** These signal a template and the customer's eyes slide off.
- **Never quote a payment or price** you have not verified with the desk. Say
  you will get them a real number, then get it.

**Never write:**
> "Thank you for your interest in our 2026 Mazda CX-5! I'd love to help you
> find your perfect vehicle. When would be a good time to discuss your needs?"

**Write:**
> "Hi Dana — [NAME] at Bob Penkhus Mazda. Saw you were looking at the CX-5
> Preferred. It's here and I can have it out front. Are you closer to a
> weekday evening or Saturday morning?"

The second one gets answered because it is obviously from a person, it proves
the car exists, and it asks one easy question.

## Step 4 — Give the video option

For any lead on a specific vehicle, add this as a second beat:

> Want me to walk out and shoot you a quick video of it? Takes me two minutes.

Personalized video gets substantially more replies than text alone, and almost
nobody at any store is doing it. If the user says yes, hand them a 5-line
shooting script: name in the first line, walk to the car, one thing they
specifically asked about, one honest flaw, invite them in.

## Step 5 — Format the output

Give the user:

1. **The message, in a copy-paste block.** No commentary above it.
2. **A one-line "why this angle"** — so they learn the pattern.
3. **The follow-up plan** — what to send and when if there is no reply.

Nothing else. They are standing on a lot with a customer walking up.

## Aged and dead leads

Different problem. They ignored somebody already, so a cheerful check-in reads
as more of the same. What works is honesty and zero pressure:

> "Hey Dana — [NAME] at Penkhus. No pitch. Just making sure you landed
> somewhere good. If you already bought, genuinely congrats — what'd you get?
> If you're still looking, I'm here."

High reply rate, and the ones who already bought frequently become referral
sources. Confirm consent before sending to anything aged.

## Follow-up cadence when there is no reply

Day 1 (immediate) → Day 2 (different channel) → Day 4 (something useful, not a
check-in — a video, a comparison, a price change) → Day 8 → Day 15 → then
monthly.

**Every touch carries new information.** "Just checking in" is not information
and it teaches people to ignore you. If there is nothing new to say, find
something: a trim came in, the incentive changed, you made a video answering
their question.
