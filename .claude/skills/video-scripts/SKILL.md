# Video Scripts

Two completely different jobs. Do not confuse them.

- **Public content** builds an audience that comes to him by name. Compounds.
- **1:1 video** converts a specific lead right now. Immediate.

He is already doing 1:1. The public side is the one with the ceiling.

---

## The rule that decides everything: education, not inventory

**"Check out this 2026 CX-5" gets no reach.** The algorithm does not distribute
it and nobody searches for it. Every salesperson in America posts it, which is
exactly why it does not work.

**What gets distribution is answering a question people actually ask:**

- "CX-5 vs CX-50 — they're the same size, so what's the difference?"
- "What a doc fee actually is and which ones are negotiable"
- "Why your trade appraisal came in lower than KBB said"
- "What 0% financing actually costs you" (it is not nothing — you give up cash)
- "Should you buy the extended warranty?"

These get saved and sent to a friend. Saves and shares are the signal the
algorithm rewards, and a save is a person who will remember his name in
three months when they are actually ready.

**Ratio: roughly 7 education / 2 local / 1 inventory.** Inventory posts are the
seasoning, never the meal.

## The Colorado Springs advantage

Local content has almost no competition and converts far better than generic
car content, because the audience is people who can actually walk into his
store. Start here:

1. **"Why your car feels slow in Colorado Springs"** — the altitude explainer.
   ~18% power loss for naturally aspirated at 6,000 ft, near zero for turbo.
   True, useful, and it establishes real expertise. **Make this one first.**
2. **"PCSing to Fort Carson? Watch this before you buy a car."** — enormous
   local audience, recurring, essentially zero competition.
3. **"Best Mazda for a Colorado winter"** — standard AWD, real snow.
4. **"Monument Hill test"** — a grade every local knows.
5. **"Turbo vs. naturally aspirated at altitude"** — the shopper-intent version
   of #1.

See `reference/colorado-springs-market.md` for the full angle list.

## Script structure — 30 to 60 seconds

```
0:00–0:02   HOOK. A question or a claim. No intro, no name, no logo.
0:02–0:10   Stakes. Why this matters to them specifically.
0:10–0:45   The answer. One idea. Concrete. A number if you have one.
0:45–0:55   The honest caveat. This is what makes people trust you.
0:55–1:00   Soft CTA. Never "come buy a car."
```

**The hook is 80% of it.** If the first two seconds do not stop a thumb,
nothing else in the video happens.

Bad hook: "Hey guys, [NAME] here at Bob Penkhus Mazda, today I wanted to talk
about..."

Good hooks:
- "Your car isn't broken. You just moved to Colorado."
- "The dealership makes more on this than on the car."
- "Everyone buys the CX-5. Most of them should've bought the CX-50."

**Always include the honest caveat.** "Here's when this is the wrong choice" is
the single highest-trust move available on camera, and it is what separates him
from every other salesperson posting.

## Output format — make batching possible

Never hand over one script. Hand over a **shoot day**: 5–10 scripts he can
knock out in two hours with one setup. Reshooting the setup is what kills
consistency, and consistency is the entire game.

For each script give:

```
TITLE:      internal name
HOOK:       exact words, first 2 seconds
LOCATION:   lot / in-car / service drive / desk
PROPS:      the specific vehicle or thing needed
SCRIPT:     spoken word for word, ~130 words per minute
B-ROLL:     3-4 specific shots to cut over
CAPTION:    posting caption, first line repeats the hook
HASHTAGS:   3-5 local + 3-5 topical, never 30
```

Group scripts by location so he shoots everything at one spot before moving.

## The 1:1 personalized video

Different animal entirely. Not content — a sales tool.

**Under 60 seconds. Shot on his phone. Never scripted word-for-word** — it must
sound unrehearsed, which is the whole point.

```
1. Say their name in the first three seconds. "Hey Dana — it's [NAME]."
2. Walk to the actual car. Show the stock tag or plate so it's provably real.
3. Answer the specific thing they asked about. Only that thing.
4. Point out one honest flaw. A scuff, the cargo number, whatever's true.
5. Two specific times. "I'm here till 8 tonight or Saturday morning."
```

Step 4 is what makes it work. Volunteering a flaw makes everything else
believable, and nobody else does it.

Shaky and real beats polished and fake. Do not let him over-produce these.

## On AI-generated video

He asked about generating video agentically. The honest read:

**Use AI for the parts that are not his face** — scripting, captions, hooks,
titles, cutting one long video into six shorts, thumbnails, B-roll.
That is real leverage and it is available today.

**Do not use an AI avatar of himself for customer-facing video.** Trust is the
entire product in car sales. Stock AI avatars convert materially worse than
real footage, disclosure rules on synthetic media are tightening, and the
downside of a customer discovering the face was fake is far worse than the
production time saved. He is already comfortable on camera — that is his
advantage over every salesperson who is not. Do not automate away the moat.

Revisit if custom-trained avatar quality clearly closes the gap. Not yet.
