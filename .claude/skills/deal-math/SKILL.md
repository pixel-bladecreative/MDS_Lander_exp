# Deal Math

The point of this skill is that "let me go check with my manager" becomes "give
me twenty seconds" — and that the number you give is defensible when the
customer gets to the desk.

**You are producing an estimate to keep a conversation moving, never a quote.**
The desk and F&I own the real numbers. Say so every time.

---

## Step 1 — Verify incentives before any math. Always.

Mazda programs change monthly, usually around the 3rd, and regional cash
differs from national.

**Search the web for the current month's Mazda incentives before calculating
anything.** Check mazdausa.com, Edmunds, or TrueCar. The snapshot in
`reference/mazda-lineup-2026.md` is dated — treat it as a starting point to
verify, not as truth.

If you cannot verify, say so and mark every number as unverified.

## Step 2 — Ask for only what changes the answer

- Vehicle, trim, and selling price (or MSRP if that is all there is)
- Down payment / trade equity
- Credit tier, roughly — this drives everything and the customer usually knows
- Term they are thinking about
- **Whether they qualify for Military ($500) or First Responder** — in Colorado
  Springs, ask this every single time. It is free money and buyers forget.

Missing pieces: assume something reasonable, label it clearly, move on.

## Step 3 — Cash vs. APR is the calculation that matters

Most customers do not understand they must choose, and most salespeople never
show them the comparison. **Show both, side by side, with the crossover point.**

```
2026 CX-90 Preferred · $42,000 selling price · $5,000 down · 60 months

  Option A — $2,000 customer cash, financed at your credit union
    Amount financed   $35,000
    Rate              6.9% (your CU quote)
    Payment           ~$692/mo
    Total interest    ~$6,520

  Option B — 1.9% Mazda Financial, no customer cash
    Amount financed   $37,000
    Rate              1.9%
    Payment           ~$646/mo
    Total interest    ~$1,760

  Option B saves ~$46/mo and ~$4,760 over the term.
  Crossover: cash wins only if you can beat ~4.5% elsewhere.
```

That table closes deals because it demonstrates you are working the customer's
side of the math instead of steering them. Run it honestly — sometimes cash
genuinely wins and you should say so.

**Always show the total interest, not just the payment.** Payment-only framing
is how customers get pushed into 84-month terms they regret, and how you lose
their next three cars.

## Step 4 — Total cost, including fuel

The CX-5's 26 mpg against hybrid rivals makes the fuel objection real. Build the honest comparison:

```
Annual fuel · 12,000 mi/yr · $3.50/gal

  CX-5           26 mpg combined    $1,615/yr    ($135/mo)
  RAV4 Hybrid    41 mpg combined    $1,024/yr    ($85/mo)
  ---------------------------------------------------------
  Difference                        ~$591/yr     (~$49/mo)
```

Then put the finance advantage next to it. If 0% for 36 saves more than $49 a
month against the customer's realistic alternative rate, the CX-5 wins on total
outlay and you can prove it. **If the customer drives 25,000 miles a year, the
hybrid genuinely wins — say so and pivot to the CX-50 Hybrid.**

Never fake this math. A customer who catches a rigged comparison is gone
permanently and tells people.

## Step 5 — Output format

Always end with:

1. **The scenario table** — clean, scannable, on the customer's side
2. **One plain-English sentence** the user can say out loud
3. **The disclaimer, every time:**
   > These are estimates for discussion. Final numbers come from the desk and
   > depend on credit approval, fees, taxes, and current program eligibility.

## Compliance — non-negotiable

- **Never produce a payment figure for public posting** without term, APR, down
  payment, and credit tier disclosed alongside it. A bare "$299/mo" in a social
  post is an advertising violation in most states.
- Never state or imply guaranteed approval.
- Never quote another dealer's pricing.
- Taxes, title, and dealer fees vary — flag them as excluded rather than
  silently omitting them.
