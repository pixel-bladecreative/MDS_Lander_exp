## The altitude argument — your best local differentiator

Colorado Springs sits at roughly **6,035 feet**. This is not trivia. It changes
which car is actually better, and almost nobody in this market sells it
correctly.

**The physics:** a naturally aspirated engine loses roughly **3% of its power
per 1,000 feet** of elevation. A turbocharged engine loses **0–1% per 1,000
feet**, because the turbo compensates for thinner air.

**At 6,000 feet:**

| Engine type | Power lost | What it feels like |
|---|---|---|
| Naturally aspirated | ~18–20% | Merging onto I-25 uphill, loaded, feels strained |
| Turbocharged | ~5% or less | Feels like it did on the test drive at sea level |

**Where this wins you deals:**

The **CX-90 and CX-70's 3.3L turbo inline-six** makes 340 hp. The Kia Telluride
and the traditional Palisade V6 are **naturally aspirated**. At Colorado Springs
elevation, a 334 hp naturally aspirated V6 is effectively delivering somewhere
near 270 hp. The Mazda turbo six is still delivering well over 320.

> "Both of these are rated around 340 horsepower — at sea level. We're at 6,000
> feet. Theirs is naturally aspirated, so it's giving up close to 20% up here.
> Mine's turbocharged, so it gives up almost nothing. That's not a spec-sheet
> argument, that's the Monument Hill grade with your family and the camper in
> back. Drive them back to back and you'll feel it in about ninety seconds."

Same argument applies to the **CX-50 Turbo, CX-30 Turbo, and Mazda3 Turbo**
against naturally aspirated rivals.

**Be honest about where it cuts against you:** the **CX-5's 2.5L is naturally
aspirated**, so it gives up the same ~18% here. And the 2026 RAV4 is hybrid-only
— an electric motor does not care about air density at all. On a CX-5 versus
RAV4 comparison at altitude, do not raise this argument. It is not your friend
on that car. Sell the CX-5 on price, interior, standard AWD, and how it drives.

**Standard AWD** is the other local layout advantage. Every Mazda crossover has
it standard; RAV4 and CR-V charge for it. In a market where people drive to
Monarch and Breckenridge and get eight inches overnight in March, that is not a
feature, it is a requirement — and you are not charging extra for it.

## The military market — the biggest single opportunity here

Colorado Springs is one of the most military-dense metros in the country:
**Fort Carson** (4th Infantry Division), **Peterson SFB**, **Schriever SFB**,
**the Air Force Academy**, and **Cheyenne Mountain**. Bob Penkhus already
markets to Fort Carson.

**Mazda Military Appreciation: $500 bonus cash** on a new 2024/2025/2026,
stackable with other offers. Eligible: active duty, reserves, retirees,
disabled veterans, and **anyone within 2 years of separation with an honorable
discharge**. Limit two per person per year. Proof required.

There is also a **First Responder & Healthcare** program — amounts vary by
dealer, typically $500–$1,000. Confirm your store's version with your desk.

### Why this is worth building a practice around

Most salespeople treat the military discount as a $500 line item they mention
at the desk. That is leaving the actual opportunity on the table.

**What a military buyer actually needs that nobody addresses:**

- **PCS timing.** Orders come with dates. Someone PCSing in 90 days has a hard
  deadline and a specific question: do I buy here or there? You can answer that.
  Peak PCS season runs roughly May through August — that is your seasonal spike
  and you should be prepared for it before it arrives, not during.
- **Deployment.** What happens to the payment, the insurance, and the car
  sitting in storage for nine months. **Servicemembers Civil Relief Act** rate
  protections are real and most buyers do not know about them.
- **First-term buyers.** Young, first real paycheck, thin credit file, and a
  well-known target for predatory lots near every base in America. Being the
  person who tells an E-3 honestly that they should not be in a $700 payment
  will cost you one deal and earn you their entire unit.
- **Separation.** "Within 2 years of separation" qualifies — nobody tells
  transitioning servicemembers this.

**The play:** become the Mazda person the base actually recommends. That is a
word-of-mouth network denser than any civilian referral pool, it turns over
constantly with new arrivals, and it responds to being treated straight more
than to any discount.

## Local competitive set

Heuberger Subaru is the entrenched Colorado Springs outdoor/AWD brand and the
reflexive default for this market. Toyota of Colorado Springs and Mike Maroone
(Honda, Chevrolet) are the volume players and actively court Fort Carson.

**Against Subaru specifically** — the most common local cross-shop — the honest
argument is interior quality, the turbo advantage at altitude on the six, and
that Mazda's AWD is standard too. Do not attack Subaru's reputation in this
town; you will lose. Position the Mazda as the one that does the same job and
feels considerably more expensive inside than it is.
