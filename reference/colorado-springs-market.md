## Local product notes

**Standard AWD across the crossover line** is the layout advantage that matters
here. RAV4 and CR-V charge for it. In a market where people drive to Monarch and
Breckenridge and get eight inches overnight in March, that is not a feature to
mention, it is the baseline requirement — and Mazda is not billing extra for it.

**Altitude and turbo behavior:** this market already understands it. Holden
sells around it and the customers here largely know it going in. Do not build
content or a pitch around explaining it — in Colorado Springs that reads as
condescending, not expert.

## The military market — the biggest single opportunity here

Colorado Springs is one of the most military-dense metros in the country:
**Fort Carson** (4th Infantry Division), **Peterson SFB**, **Schriever SFB**,
**the Air Force Academy**, and **Cheyenne Mountain**. Bob Penkhus already
markets to Fort Carson.

**Mazda Military Appreciation: $500 bonus cash** on a new 2024/2025/2026,
stackable with other offers. Eligible: active duty, reserves, retirees,
disabled veterans, and **anyone within 2 years of separation with an honorable
discharge**. Limit two per person per year. Proof required.

There is also a **First Responder & Healthcare** program — amounts vary by
dealer, typically $500–$1,000. Confirm your store's version with your desk.

### Why this is worth building a practice around

Most salespeople treat the military discount as a $500 line item mentioned at
the desk. That leaves the actual opportunity untouched.

**What a military buyer needs that nobody addresses:**

- **PCS timing.** Orders come with dates. Someone PCSing in 90 days has a hard
  deadline and one specific question: do I buy here or there? You can answer
  that. Peak PCS season runs roughly May through August — that is the seasonal
  spike, and the practice needs to exist before it arrives, not during.
- **Deployment.** What happens to the payment, the insurance, and a car sitting
  in storage for nine months. **Servicemembers Civil Relief Act** rate
  protections are real and most buyers have never heard of them.
- **First-term buyers.** Young, first real paycheck, thin credit file, and the
  known target of every predatory lot outside every base in America. Being the
  person who tells an E-3 honestly that he should not be in a $700 payment will
  cost one deal and earn his entire unit.
- **Separation.** "Within 2 years of separation" qualifies, and essentially
  nobody tells transitioning servicemembers this.

**The play:** become the Mazda person the base actually recommends. That is a
word-of-mouth network denser than any civilian referral pool, it turns over
constantly with new arrivals, and it responds to being treated straight far
more than to any discount.

## Local content angles

Hyper-local, genuinely searched, and almost no competition:

1. **"PCSing to Fort Carson? Watch this before you buy a car."** — high intent,
   recurring audience, essentially zero competition. Make this one first.
2. **"What the military discount actually stacks with"** — most people assume
   it replaces other offers. It does not.
3. **"Best Mazda for a Colorado winter"** — standard AWD, ground clearance,
   real snow footage.
4. **Ski-trip loading** — cargo, roof boxes, real gear, Ute Pass. The CX-50's
   entire personality.
5. **"Deploying? Here's what to do with your car and your loan."** — SCRA,
   storage, insurance. Nobody in this market has made it.
6. **CX-5 vs CX-50** — same size, constantly confused, high search volume.

## Local competitive set

Heuberger Subaru is the entrenched Colorado Springs outdoor/AWD brand and the
reflexive default in this market. Toyota of Colorado Springs and Mike Maroone
(Honda, Chevrolet) are the volume players and actively court Fort Carson.

**Against Subaru specifically** — the most common local cross-shop — the honest
argument is interior quality, the inline-six in the CX-90/CX-70 against
anything Subaru offers, and that Mazda's AWD is standard too. Do not attack
Subaru's reputation in this town; you will lose that fight. Position the Mazda
as the one that does the same job and feels considerably more expensive inside
than it costs.
