# Compliance Guardrails

Read once. Then let the skills enforce it.

This is the part of the playbook that protects you personally. Everything else
here is about making money; this is about not losing it.

---

## TCPA — the one that can actually hurt you

Statutory damages run **$500 to $1,500 per message**. Not per campaign — per
message. Auto dealers are a favored target for plaintiff firms, and **the
burden of proving consent falls on you**, not on the person suing.

### The line that matters

| You are doing this | Status |
|---|---|
| Replying to someone who just submitted a lead form on your inventory | **Fine.** They initiated. |
| Replying to someone who texted you first | **Fine.** |
| Texting a past customer you have an existing relationship with, one to one, by hand | **Generally fine.** Keep it conversational, honor any opt-out instantly. |
| Blasting a list of "lost" leads from 6 months ago with a promotion | **Do not.** |
| Texting a purchased or scraped list | **Do not. Ever.** |
| Automated or bulk marketing texts to anyone without written consent on file | **Do not.** |

**Prior Express Written Consent (PEWC)** is what marketing texts require. It
means the person affirmatively agreed — checked an unchecked box, submitted a
form with clear disclosure, or texted a keyword — to receive marketing texts
**from your dealership specifically**, and that agreement is logged with source,
timestamp, and scope.

### The working rules

1. **Conversational beats promotional.** A one-to-one message answering a
   person's actual question is a different animal from a blast. Write like a
   human talking to one person, which you should be doing anyway.
2. **Check the CRM consent field before you send.** Every time. If it is blank
   or says opted out, you do not send. No exceptions, no "just this once."
3. **Honor opt-outs immediately and everywhere.** One "stop" kills text, email,
   and calls. Not just the channel it came in on.
4. **Never automate outbound to a cold list.** Automation is exactly what turns
   one bad message into a class action.
5. **When in doubt, call instead.** A phone call to an existing customer sits
   on far safer ground than a text to a stranger.

### If you are ever unsure

Ask your GM or the store's compliance contact. "I wasn't sure so I asked" is a
sentence that has never ended anyone's career. Guessing has.

## Advertising claims

You have free rein on your personal accounts. That freedom ends at the
factual claim.

- **Never post a price or payment without the disclosure your store uses.**
  Payment examples need term, APR, down payment, and credit tier. "$299/mo" on
  its own is an advertising violation in most states.
- **Never say "lowest price," "best deal in town," or "guaranteed approval."**
  Unprovable superlatives and credit guarantees draw regulator attention.
- **Do not post another dealer's pricing** to compare against.
- **Stock photos of a car you don't have in stock, presented as your
  inventory,** is a bait-and-switch problem. Shoot the actual unit.
- **Say "see dealer for details"** and link to the real VDP.

## Customer privacy

- Never post a customer's face, name, plate, or paperwork without explicit
  permission. Get it in writing — a text saying "yes you can post it" is fine.
- **Never photograph or screenshot a credit application, a deal jacket, or
  anything with a SSN, DOB, or account number.** That is Gramm-Leach-Bliley
  territory and it is a fireable offense at every store in America.
- Delivery photos: ask first, every time. Most people say yes and love it.

## Using AI — disclose where it matters

You are going to use Claude to draft messages and scripts. That is fine and
nobody needs a disclaimer on a text you wrote with help.

Two places it does matter:

1. **Never let AI send anything on its own.** You read every message before it
   goes out. The moment an automated system is texting customers without you
   in the loop, you have an autodialer problem under the TCPA and a quality
   problem with your name on it.
2. **If you ever use AI-generated video or voice of yourself,** label it.
   Synthetic-media disclosure rules are tightening, and in a trust business the
   downside of being caught faking a face is worse than any efficiency you gain.

## Your own reputation

Anything you post lives forever and your GM will eventually see it. Before you
hit publish, ask whether you would be comfortable with the customer in the
video, your sales manager, and the Mazda factory rep all watching it.

If the answer is no, it is not the post that makes you money.
