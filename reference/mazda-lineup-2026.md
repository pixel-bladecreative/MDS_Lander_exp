staleness rule at the bottom before quoting anything from this file.

## CX-5 — the volume car, and the hardest sell

**2026 is a new generation.** 4.5 inches longer, 2.3 inches more rear knee
room. Starts at $29,990 ($31,485 delivered).

**Where it wins:**
- **Price.** Undercuts the RAV4 by ~$1,900 and the CR-V by ~$930 to start.
- **Standard AWD.** Both rivals charge for it.
- **Interior.** Genuinely near-luxury materials at a mainstream price. This is
  the thing that closes it — get them to sit in it.
- **How it drives.** Nobody cross-shopping cares until they drive it. Then some
  of them care a lot. The test drive is not optional on this car.

**Where it loses — know this cold:**
- **Fuel economy, badly.** 26 mpg combined, gas-only. The 2026 RAV4 is
  hybrid-only at 41–43 mpg. The CR-V Hybrid is 37. This is a real gap, not a
  spin problem.
- **Cargo.** 33.7 cu ft behind the second row / 66.5 max. CR-V is 39.3 / 76.5.
- **No hybrid until 2027.**

**How to handle the mpg gap honestly:** Do the math out loud instead of
deflecting. At 12,000 miles a year and $3.50/gal, 26 mpg vs 41 mpg is roughly
$590 a year — about $49 a month. Then put the incentive next to it: 0% for 36
months against a competitor's market rate is usually worth more than $49 a
month, and it is money they keep whether gas goes up or down. If the customer
drives 25,000 miles a year, the hybrid genuinely wins and you should say so —
then sell them a CX-50 Hybrid instead of losing the deal to a RAV4.

## CX-90 / CX-70 — the strongest position in the lineup

**CX-90 starts at $38,800.** 3.3L turbo inline-six making 340 hp / 369 lb-ft,
or the PHEV, both with 8-speed auto and standard AWD.

**Versus Telluride and Palisade:**
- More power than the 2027 Telluride (340 vs 334 hp) at a lower base price.
- Standard AWD; the Koreans charge for it.
- 25 mpg combined vs the Palisade V6's 20.
- Straight sixes and rear-biased AWD are a luxury-brand layout at a mainstream
  price. Nobody else in the segment offers it.

**Where it loses:** Third-row cargo is 14.9 cu ft vs the Telluride's 21. If
they are hauling six people and luggage, the Telluride is the better tool and
they will figure that out eventually. Qualify early: "Is the third row for
everyday, or for the occasional extra kid?" Occasional-use buyers pick the CX-90
almost every time once they drive it.

**The CX-70** is the same vehicle without the third row. The buyer is an empty
nester or a dog owner who wants the inline-six and a huge flat cargo floor.
Most stores forget to show it. It is often sitting on the lot longer, which
means more room to deal.

## Where the money is right now

**The CX-70 and CX-90 PHEVs are carrying the heaviest support in the lineup:
$5,000 customer cash plus $1,000 dealer bonus, or 0% financing up to 72
months.** That is the most dramatic payment story you can tell this month, and
it is a far better content and outreach hook than another CX-5 walkaround that
every other salesperson in the country is also posting.

Do not let the PHEV explain itself. Most shoppers do not understand that it
drives on electricity for daily commuting and switches to gas for road trips
with no range anxiety and no charger dependency. That explanation, delivered
in 45 seconds, is a video that will outperform anything else you post.

### Staleness rule — this is not optional

Before you quote a number to a customer or put one in a video, **confirm it
against your dealer's current program sheet or mazdausa.com.** If you are
using Claude, tell it to search for the current month's Mazda incentives
before it does any payment math. Quoting a dead incentive is how you lose a
deal at the desk and how a happy customer becomes an angry one.

Regional programs vary. Your store's dealer cash may differ from national.
Your desk is the authority, not this file and not the internet.
